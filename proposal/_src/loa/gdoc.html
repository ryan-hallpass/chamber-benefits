EXHIBIT A

Scope of Work

The following Scope of Work (“SOW”) outlines the services Hall Pass Digital, LLC (“Hallpass”) will provide to the Ardmore Chamber of Commerce (“Chamber”) during the contract term. This scope corresponds to the Growth-level package previously defined by Hallpass, combining full content production and channel management with the engagement and measurement infrastructure of the Essentials tier.

The scope below is built to execute against the Chamber’s published Economic Vision Plan. Goal 5 of that plan calls for the Chamber to “develop strategic, data-driven marketing to overcome capacity constraints,” and names expanded social media, increased earned media, and enhanced online messaging as its tactics. Part One of this Agreement delivers that capacity. Sections 8 and 9 extend it into Goal 3 (downtown development and entrepreneurship) and Goal 1 (target industry marketing and workforce), respectively.

PART ONE — CONTENT & CHANNEL MANAGEMENT

1. Social Media Content & Management

Hallpass will provide comprehensive social media content creation, publishing, and management services on behalf of the Chamber, focused on the Ardmore business community.

Strategy

  • Annual content calendar with recommended posting frequency, seasonal campaigns, and key themes
  • Maintenance of established brand voice, messaging pillars, and visual guidelines
  • Quarterly reviews and adjustments based on performance insights

Content Creation

  • Instagram: 4–6 posts per month
  • TikTok: 4–6 videos per month
  • LinkedIn: 4 posts per month focused on business and economic activity
  • Facebook: 4–6 posts per month
  • YouTube Shorts: repurposed and original edits
  • Reposting of earned media

Creative Services

  • Video scripting, storyboarding, and voiceover production
  • Custom image and design creation
  • Editing and integration of new and existing b-roll
  • Engagement-focused caption writing

Account Management

  • Management of Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts
  • Publishing, scheduling, and engagement management
  • Cross-platform optimization tailored to each channel

Reporting

  • Monthly performance reports (engagement, reach, audience growth, conversions)
  • Monthly check-ins for alignment and idea generation
  • Rapid-response support for urgent opportunities (within 48 hours when feasible)

2. Content Production Shoots

Hallpass will conduct three (3) multi-day content production shoots over the course of the engagement. Each shoot will include:

  • Pre-production planning, scheduling, and location coordination
  • Filming across multiple Ardmore locations, including on-site at member businesses
  • Coordination with business owners, local ambassadors, and lifestyle creators
  • Capture of high-quality content for ongoing campaigns
  • Editing deliverables in multiple formats (short-form, story, and long-form social cuts)
  • Captioning, formatting, and creation of custom thumbnails

All raw footage and edited content will be delivered to the Chamber for unlimited promotional use.

PART TWO — SMALL BUSINESS ENGAGEMENT & MEASUREMENT SYSTEM

The sections below are designed around a single organizing principle: every dollar the Chamber spends promoting its members should produce a number that can be shown back to that member at renewal, and shown to a prospect during recruitment.

3. Small Business Support: Discount Card Engagement & Tracking

The discount card is one of the Chamber’s most direct mechanisms for driving paying customers into member businesses. It serves two audiences at once: residents who use it to discover businesses in their own town, and visitors who use it to decide where to spend money while they are here. In its current form, however, participating businesses have no way to see what the program produced for them, and neither audience can be told apart from the other. This section converts the discount card from a static promotional item into a trackable customer-acquisition channel that demonstrates measurable return for participating small businesses — and that distinguishes resident demand from visitor demand.

Deliverables include:

  • Tracking framework for QR scans, discount-card page visits, consumer interactions, and offer engagement
  • Resident vs. visitor attribution — where technically feasible, distinguishing local from out-of-town engagement, so the Chamber can report tourism-driven spending separately from resident spending
  • Coordination with the Chamber and/or the Chamber’s contracted web-development vendor to connect the discount-card experience to email capture and measurement
  • Email capture flow tied to the discount-card experience, subject to final platform and privacy requirements, feeding the segmented audience described in Section 4
  • “Claim this offer” tracking, or similar offer-engagement tracking, so the Chamber can identify which offers and which participating member businesses are generating customer interest
  • Per-business engagement attribution — isolating results by participating member so each business can be shown what the program delivered for them specifically
  • Integration of tracking data into the broader dashboard and reporting system

Recruitment value: The per-business results produced by this system become the Chamber’s most concrete recruitment asset. Rather than describing membership benefits in the abstract, the Chamber will be able to show a prospective member documented figures — scans, claims, referral traffic, and new customer contacts delivered to comparable businesses in their category. These outcomes are surfaced to prospects and existing members through the reporting described in Sections 6 and 7.

4. Small Business Support: CRM & Marketing Automation

Most Chamber member businesses are small enough that they have no marketing staff and no owned audience of their own. This section builds and operates a consumer email audience on the Chamber’s behalf, which the Chamber can then direct toward its member businesses — effectively providing small members with distribution they could not build independently.

The audience is built as a single list with intent-based segments, so that residents, visitors, and prospective relocators can each receive relevant content from one operating system rather than three separate programs. Visitor-facing subscribers acquired through the discount card and tourism channels are captured and segmented here, and are served by the same infrastructure that serves the resident audience.

Deliverables include:

  • CRM setup and email list structure for consumer, visitor, and community subscribers
  • Signup source tracking where technically feasible, including discount-card source attribution
  • Automated welcome-to-Ardmore email sequence for new subscribers, branched by intent segment
  • Email templates for event visibility, business recommendations, offer reminders, and seasonal campaigns
  • Segmentation by intent (resident, visitor, prospective relocator), industry, geography, and engagement level, so promotion can be targeted to the audiences most relevant to each business
  • Basic list hygiene and audience-growth tracking

Recruitment value: Total audience size and per-business referral performance become quantifiable membership benefits. A prospective member can be shown the size of the consumer audience the Chamber can put their business in front of, and what comparable businesses received from that distribution.

Note: Member-facing lifecycle automation — new member onboarding, win-back, and exit survey sequences — is scoped under the Member Engagement & Visibility Agreement dated May 12, 2026 and is not duplicated or re-billed here. The consumer audience built under this Agreement is a distinct list serving a distinct purpose.

5. Event Visibility System — What’s Happening Around Ardmore

Hallpass will operate a push-based event visibility system, published under the title What’s Happening Around Ardmore, designed to get Chamber, member, and community events in front of subscribers before they decide where to spend their time and money. This includes:

  • Weekly What’s Happening Around Ardmore email to the Chamber’s consumer, visitor, and community subscriber list, with content emphasis varied by intent segment
  • Collaboration with Chamber staff to populate upcoming event content
  • Automated monitoring of key event sources, venues, and public calendars identified by the Chamber, with collected listings staged for Chamber review
  • Member-business event listings surfaced alongside community events, with outbound links routed through the tracked redirect described in Section 7 so each member can be shown the traffic the email delivered
  • Email copy, layout, scheduling, and basic performance review
  • Chamber review and approval before outgoing sends

The Chamber is responsible for timely review/approval of email content and for confirming event information accuracy before publication.

6. Analytics Reporting & Measurement Dashboard

Hallpass will provide a monthly analytics dashboard and reporting framework to help the Chamber measure the effectiveness of member engagement and marketing efforts. Tracked metrics include:

  • QR scans and offer-page traffic
  • New subscribers and total list growth
  • Offer claims and other offer-engagement actions
  • Email sends, opens, clicks, and subscriber growth
  • Member business profile engagement
  • Social content performance across all managed channels
  • Other benchmark data only if the Chamber provides the data or approves a reliable source

Dashboard metrics depend on available platform data, proper access, and the technical feasibility of tracking the underlying member and consumer actions.

Optimization: Hallpass will provide ongoing recommendations based on dashboard performance, list growth, offer engagement, event email performance, and social channel results, delivered in writing alongside the monthly report.

Recruitment value: The dashboard produces the Chamber’s year-over-year proof of impact — total customer engagements driven to member businesses, audience reach delivered, and offer claims generated across the membership. These aggregate figures support membership recruitment campaigns, board reporting, and the annual meeting.

7. Business Spotlight Integration & Visibility Data Feed

The digital member business profiles, the member directory, and the Annual Visibility Report are created and hosted under the separate Member Engagement & Visibility Agreement dated May 12, 2026. This Agreement does not recreate them and does not bill for them. What it does is put those assets in front of a consumer audience and supply them with performance data they would otherwise lack. This includes:

  • Discount card spotlight integration — Hallpass will coordinate with the Chamber and/or the Chamber’s contracted web-development vendor to feature selected existing member business profiles within the discount card experience, so that a consumer can preview a participating business before they visit. Hallpass’s role is coordination, specification, and content delivery; technical implementation on the discount card website is performed by the Chamber’s contracted vendor.
  • Profile promotion across managed channels — routing traffic to existing member profiles through the social content, event emails, and offer campaigns operated under this Agreement
  • Tracked outbound attribution — all outbound links to member businesses published under this Agreement, including job listings under Section 9, are routed through a Chamber-owned tracked redirect. This adds no friction for the visitor and allows every click to be attributed to a named member business. Without this mechanism, outbound engagement cannot be measured or reported at the member level.
  • Per-business engagement data feed — delivery of the tracked figures produced under Sections 3 through 6 (discount-card scans, offer claims, referral traffic, profile page views, email-driven engagement) in a per-member format suitable for direct use in the Annual Visibility Report
  • Chamber-level rollup summary — an aggregate view of the same figures, suitable for board presentation, annual meeting, and membership recruitment

Recruitment value: The Annual Visibility Report is only as persuasive as the numbers in it. Without the tracking infrastructure in this Agreement, that report documents mentions and features; with it, the report documents scans, claims, referral traffic, and customer contacts delivered to a named business. This Agreement is what converts the Chamber’s existing reporting instrument from a record of activity into a record of results.

Note: Any hours billed by the Chamber’s contracted web-development vendor for integration work on the discount card website or other Chamber web properties are the responsibility of the Chamber, not Hallpass.

PART THREE — ENTREPRENEUR SUPPORT & WORKFORCE VISIBILITY

The Chamber’s Economic Vision Plan sets two objectives this Agreement can act on directly: accelerating entrepreneurship (Goal 3) and responding to the county’s manufacturing contraction (Goal 1). The sections below deliver against both, and are built to run largely on automated collection so that ongoing cost stays flat as volume grows.

8. Small Business Digital Readiness Program

The Chamber’s Economic Vision Plan reports a 10.3% increase in local business applications, with the State of Oklahoma committing $1 million to entrepreneurship initiatives. The great majority of those ventures launch with no marketing staff and no owned audience. This section gives the Chamber a scalable way to make them more competitive, and gives every existing member a measurable reason to stay.

Part A — Training library. Hallpass will produce and maintain an on-demand video library for Chamber members covering:

  • Search fundamentals — how local customers find a business, and what makes a listing rank
  • Google Business Profile and online reviews — claiming, completing, and actively generating reviews
  • Social media best practices for small business, adapted from Hallpass’s established playbooks
  • Practical AI in the workplace — applied uses for small business operations, marketing, and customer service

The library launches with a minimum of twelve modules and is expanded quarterly. Modules are hosted for member access and remain available throughout the term.

Part B — Digital Readiness Scorecard. Hallpass will operate an automated audit of each member business’s public digital presence, producing a per-member readiness score covering:

  • Google Business Profile claimed, complete, and accurate
  • Review volume and average rating relative to category
  • Website presence and mobile usability
  • Published hours, contact details, and category accuracy
  • Active social presence

Each member receives a personalised score with the specific library modules that address their weakest areas. Scores are re-run quarterly so movement is visible and reportable. Scoring depends on data being publicly available; businesses with no discoverable online presence are reported as such, which is itself an actionable finding for Chamber staff.

Recruitment and retention value: The scorecard converts “the Chamber helps your business” from a claim into a number that moves. It gives Chamber staff a specific, non-sales reason to contact every member each quarter, and it produces aggregate improvement figures suitable for the Annual Visibility Report and for board reporting.

9. Now Hiring in Ardmore — Workforce Visibility System

Following the wind-down of tire production at Michelin, Carter County lost roughly a third of its manufacturing employment in a single year, and the Economic Vision Plan sets a corresponding priority on target industry marketing and workforce. Chamber member businesses are hiring throughout this period, but their openings are scattered across a dozen platforms. This section consolidates them into a single Chamber-owned channel.

Collection. Openings are gathered automatically rather than submitted. Hallpass will operate weekly collection from:

  • CareerOneStop, the national job bank sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor’s Employment and Training Administration, filtered to the Ardmore area
  • Structured job data published by employers on their own career sites, including public applicant-tracking feeds and schema.org JobPosting markup
  • Direct submission by any member business whose postings are not publicly discoverable, via a single email address requiring no account or login

Because listings are collected from live sources rather than submitted and maintained by hand, filled positions drop off automatically when the employer closes them. Directly submitted listings expire after thirty days unless renewed. No member business is required to log in, post, or close out anything.

Distribution. Collected openings are published through:

  • A weekly “Now Hiring in Ardmore” email to the Chamber’s community subscriber list, organised by category, with Chamber member businesses visibly identified
  • Social distribution across the channels managed under Section 1
  • A Chamber-hosted archive page, searchable and filterable, updated weekly and built for search visibility on local hiring queries

All outbound links route through the tracked redirect described in Section 7, so click volume is attributable to each named employer and reportable to that member.

Recruitment value: Ardmore’s largest employers are among the Chamber’s largest dues payers, and this delivers them free, recurring local hiring reach they cannot otherwise buy. It also surfaces, week after week, exactly which Ardmore businesses are hiring and are not Chamber members — a continuously refreshed, self-maintaining membership prospect list.

Scope note: Hallpass does not warrant that every opening at every Ardmore employer will be captured. Some employers publish job data in forms that cannot be collected automatically, and some prohibit automated collection outright; those employers are invited to submit directly. Hallpass will not collect from any source whose terms of service prohibit it. The Chamber’s community subscriber list is established under Section 4 of this Agreement and will grow over the course of the term; distribution reach in the early months reflects that.
